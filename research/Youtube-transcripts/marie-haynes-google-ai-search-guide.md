# What I Think About Google's "Optimizing Your Website For Generative AI Features On Google Search" Guidance
**Speaker:** Dr. Marie Haynes
**Publication Date:** May 15, 2026
**YouTube URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hOl5BYYkMxs

---
## Transcript Summary
- Marie walks through Google's newly published official guide (released the same day) on optimizing websites for AI Overviews and AI Mode, covering it section by section.
- She explains Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG): Google's AI features do not generate answers from training data alone but retrieve and ground responses in indexed, crawlable web pages — meaning traditional indexing and crawlability remain foundational.
- She details "query fan-out," the technique where a specialized model generates multiple concurrent sub-queries from a single user question, and warns against producing large volumes of content purely to target anticipated fan-out queries, since this risks becoming "commodity content."
- Marie emphasizes Google's central distinction between "commodity content" (generic, widely duplicated information) and "non-commodity content" (unique viewpoints and first-hand experience), tying this directly to the E-E-A-T framework's "Experience" pillar.
- She shares Danny Sullivan's practical test for content quality: if a page disappeared from the web entirely, would anyone miss it?
- She discusses Google's guidance dismissing several popular GEO tactics as unnecessary: LLMs.txt files, content chunking, and heavy structured-data/schema investment for informational (non-e-commerce) content.
- Technical SEO is reframed as a threshold requirement (crawlability, indexability, rendering) rather than a primary ranking lever, except for e-commerce, where page speed still affects conversion.
- She covers emerging "agentic experiences" — Google's Business Agent feature in Merchant Center, the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP), and WebMCP — describing how AI agents perceive and interact with sites via screenshots, HTML, and the accessibility tree.
- She closes by noting that "inauthentic mentions" (manipulative guest posts, roundups, and link building) are explicitly flagged in the new Google guidance as counterproductive for both traditional and AI-driven search visibility.
