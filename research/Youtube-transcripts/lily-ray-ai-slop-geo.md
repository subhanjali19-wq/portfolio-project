## Lily Ray on AI Slop, GEO, and What Actually Works  
 
**Speaker:** Lily Ray (interviewd by Ross Hudgens)
**URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DKAGf2lk488  
**Publication date**: May 13, 2026  
**Channel**: Content and Conversation (Siege Media, hosted by Ross Hudgens)

## Transcript Summary
- Ray discussed launching her own consulting practice, Algorythmic, while continuing her VP role at Amsive.
- She walked through the rise and likely decline of self-promotional listicles and comparison/alternative pages as GEO tactics.
- She described how Google and Microsoft have begun explicitly flagging certain GEO tactics — including prompt injection via "summarize with AI" buttons — as spam rather than gray-area optimization.
- Ray shared a specific data point: around January 20, 2026, she identified dozens of companies (one with roughly 2,000 self-promotional articles) hit by a targeted-looking action against listicle-heavy content.
- She raised a regulatory angle most SEOs overlook: FTC transparency rules require substantiated proof when a company claims competitive superiority — a bar most scaled "best X" content fails to meet.
- Ray pushed back on the idea that traditional SEO rankings and AI citation are separable, arguing strong organic rankings remain foundational to AI visibility.
- She detailed her team's day-to-day AI stack and what a "technical GEO checklist" looks like heading into the second half of 2026.
- She closed by framing the central unresolved industry question as economic: how Google reconciles user demand for instant AI answers with an ad-revenue model built on click-throughs.

**Main lessons**:
- Short-term AI content-scaling tactics tend to follow a boom-then-crackdown cycle — treat rapid, easy wins as a red flag, not a strategy.
- Traditional SEO fundamentals (rankings, indexation, quality) remain the foundation AI search visibility is built on, not a separate track.
- Regulatory exposure (e.g., FTC substantiation requirements) is an underappreciated risk in AI-generated comparison/"best of" content.
