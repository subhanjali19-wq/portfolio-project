# LinkedIn Posts

## Post 1
**Title:** How to Build a Representative AI Search Prompt Library  
**Publication Date:** June 8, 2026  
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aleyda_how-to-build-a-representative-ai-search-activity-7469723521947131905-6DVK/  
**Summary:** Aleyda argues that most teams measuring AI search visibility use the wrong prompt sets — either tool-default prompts or a quick informal list — which produces inaccurate visibility data from the start. She lays out a structured method for building a representative, non-exhaustive prompt library tied to actual audience language and buyer journeys.

**Key Takeaways**
- Visibility tracking is only as good as the prompt set behind it; unrepresentative prompts produce misleading data.
- Prompt libraries should be representative and structured (30–50 prompts grouped by topic/journey stage), not exhaustive or random.
- Visibility gaps should be diagnosed by cause — content gap, source credibility issue, or entity recognition problem — since each requires a different fix.

## Post 2
**Title:** Screaming Frog v24 MCP Release — Practical Take  
**Publication Date:** May 31, 2026 
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aleyda_a-new-release-from-screaming-frog-with-activity-7462557149177733120-U2MT  
**Summary:** Aleyda reacted to Screaming Frog v24's new built-in MCP (Model Context Protocol) server, which lets AI tools trigger crawls and pull data via natural language instead of manual CSV exports. She flagged it as a genuinely useful AI + SEO workflow update, while cautioning about API credit costs and the continued need to validate AI-assisted outputs.

**Key Takeaways**
- MCP integration removes manual export/import friction between crawlers and AI tools, speeding up mechanical audit work.
- AI-assisted workflows don't replace SEO judgment — they free up time for prioritization and analysis.
- Output from AI-connected tools still needs to be validated, especially when merging multiple data sources.

## Post 3
**Title:** The State of Ecommerce SEO & AI Search in 2026 (SMX Munich recap)  
**Publication Date:** Approximate (March 2026)  
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aleyda_the-state-of-ecommerce-seo-ai-search-activity-7437200802991067136-w0Pq  
**Summary:** Recap of her SMX Munich talk on how AI platforms evaluate ecommerce content by matching jobs-to-be-done against constraints (price, size, compatibility, etc.) rather than matching simple keywords. She recommends mapping product content and filters to natural-language constraints instead of generic "best X" targeting.

**Key Takeaways**
- AI platforms reason over jobs-to-be-done × constraints, not flat keyword lists — content and filters should be structured accordingly.
- Ecommerce sites should audit "topic completeness" (percentage of product attributes, use cases, and decision scenarios actually covered).
- Traditional SEO fundamentals remain necessary but need restructuring, not replacement, for AI search.
