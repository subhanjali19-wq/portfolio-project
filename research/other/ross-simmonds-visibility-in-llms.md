# Visibility In LLMs: How Distribution Opens The Gates To LLM Citations

**Publication Date:** March 27, 2026
**URL:** https://rosssimmonds.com/blog/visibility-llms/

## Summary
In this blog post, Ross Simmonds argues that "AI visibility" is not a new discipline but a continuation of long-term distribution work: brands winning citations in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews today are largely benefiting from SEO, community presence, and review-site investments made years earlier. He reframes Google itself as the most widely used AI system, since AI Overviews now synthesize and cite sources for a large share of everyday searches, collapsing the distinction between traditional SEO and Generative Engine Optimization (GEO).

The post lays out a four-part "distribution stack" for earning LLM citations: (1) Reddit, citing third-party research showing it as the single most-cited domain across AI platforms and referencing Foundation's internal analysis of 8,566 B2B SaaS keywords; (2) YouTube, which the post says recently overtook Reddit in share of LLM answer citations due to improved transcript and metadata parsing; (3) review platforms such as G2, Capterra, and Trustpilot, valued for their verified-credibility signal; and (4) offsite mentions and digital PR, including guest posts and "top X" list placements. Simmonds closes by stressing that this distribution work compounds over time and that the competitive window for building an LLM citation advantage is narrowing.

## Why This Resource Matters
This post is a strong example of applied AI-SEO/GEO content production: it combines original and third-party research data, a clear actionable framework, and direct relevance to how brands should structure content and distribution for AI search visibility. It reflects Simmonds's core thesis — that distribution, not just content creation, determines AI citation outcomes — making it a useful primary source for understanding current GEO practice.
