# Stop Chasing Reddit and Wikipedia: What Actually Drives AI Recommendations

**Type:** Blog / Column (Search Engine Land)
**Publication Date:** March 27, 2026
**URL:** https://searchengineland.com/reddit-wikipedia-what-drives-ai-recommendations-472580

## Summary
In this Search Engine Land column, Gaetano DiNardi challenges the widely circulated industry narrative that brands should chase visibility on Reddit and Wikipedia because those domains are the most-cited sources across major LLM platforms. He argues that pivoting an entire GEO strategy toward these two platforms is a "massive strategic error" for most B2B brands, since the data underlying the trend is frequently misread. He cites Semrush research showing that up to 80% of Reddit threads cited by AI systems have fewer than 20 upvotes, and that the average cited post is roughly 900 days old — indicating that LLMs surface established, historical consensus rather than manufactured or recent virality.

DiNardi extends the argument to Wikipedia, applying the same logic that "hacking" these platforms for AI citations misunderstands how large language models actually select and weight sources. He connects this to the broader theme of his other 2026 work (including "Why GEO Is a Reputation Problem" and "The Dark SEO Funnel"): that AI visibility is fundamentally driven by brand positioning, category alignment, and entity strength across the web — not by technical tricks, chunking, or platform-specific SEO hacks aimed at gaming LLM citation behavior.
